🎯 Our Philosophy: High-Risk Only

Security teams are overwhelmed with 30,000+ CVEs published annually. This platform cuts through the noise by focusing on vulnerabilities that actually matter: those with proven exploitation likelihood (EPSS ≥60%) and severe impact (CRITICAL/HIGH severity).

Why This Works: Our 24.7% CISA KEV overlap validates that EPSS ≥60% threshold effectively predicts real-world exploitation. The remaining 75.3% are high-risk CVEs likely to be exploited soon but not yet cataloged by CISA.

🔍 Filtering Criteria

1. EPSS Threshold: ≥60% Exploitation Probability

The Exploit Prediction Scoring System (EPSS) is a data-driven model developed by FIRST.org that predicts the probability of a CVE being exploited in the wild within 30 days.

Rationale: Research shows that only 2-7% of published CVEs are ever exploited. EPSS ≥60% represents the top ~5% of vulnerabilities most likely to be actively targeted. This threshold balances comprehensive coverage (catching emerging threats) with actionable volume (manageable for security teams).

EPSS Distribution in Our Dataset:

  • Minimum EPSS: 60.25% (threshold enforcement verified)
  • Maximum EPSS: 94.58% (extremely high exploitation likelihood)
  • Average EPSS: 83.3% (significantly above industry average of ~1%)

2. Severity Filtering: CRITICAL and HIGH Only

We exclusively track CVEs with CVSS base scores indicating severe impact:

  • CRITICAL CVSS 9.0-10.0 (185 CVEs, 62.7%)
  • HIGH CVSS 7.0-8.9 (110 CVEs, 37.3%)
Why No MEDIUM Severity? While MEDIUM CVEs can be important in specific contexts, our focus on EPSS ≥60% inherently filters for high-impact vulnerabilities. MEDIUM severity CVEs rarely achieve ≥60% EPSS unless they have exceptional exploitation characteristics (e.g., wormable, pre-auth RCE), which would typically elevate their CVSS score to HIGH/CRITICAL anyway.

4. SSVC (Stakeholder-Specific Vulnerability Categorization)

Every CVE receives SSVC scoring based on the CISA-developed decision framework. SSVC is a more nuanced approach to vulnerability prioritization that considers decision factors beyond just CVSS severity, focusing on exploitation reality and stakeholder impact.

SSVC Philosophy: Not all CRITICAL-severity vulnerabilities require immediate patching. SSVC helps organizations make risk-based decisions by evaluating exploitation status, automatable attack chains, and technical impact—resulting in three actionable priority tiers: ACT (24h), ATTEND (14d), TRACK (standard cycle).

Compact Notation Explained

SSVC uses a compact notation format to quickly communicate decision factors: 

Format: [E]/[A]/[T]
  E = Exploitation (A=active, P=poc, N=none)
  A = Automatable (Y=yes, N=no)
  T = Technical Impact (T=total, P=partial)

Examples:
  A/Y/T → Exploitation:active, Automatable:yes, Impact:total = ACT tier
  A/N/T → Exploitation:active, Automatable:no, Impact:total = ATTEND tier
  P/Y/T → Exploitation:poc, Automatable:yes, Impact:total = ATTEND tier
  N/N/P → Exploitation:none, Automatable:no, Impact:partial = TRACK tier

SSVC Data Sources & Inference Logic

SSVC metrics are extracted from two sources:

  1. Official CISA-ADP Containers (Direct Extraction): When CISA has manually assessed a CVE, SSVC metrics are embedded in the CVE 5.0 JSON under the containers.adp[].metrics[].ssvc field. These are considered authoritative.
  2. Automated Inference (Fallback Engine): For CVEs without CISA-ADP data, we infer SSVC metrics using multi-source intelligence:
    • Exploitation: CISA KEV catalog, EPSS ≥80%, PoC detection in references
    • Automatable: CVSS vector analysis (AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N)
    • Technical Impact: CVSS scope change + CIA triad (S:C OR C:H/I:H/A:H)
Validation Results: We validated SSVC accuracy against the CISA KEV catalog (1,442 officially confirmed exploited vulnerabilities). Our SSVC extraction achieved:
  • 100% Accuracy - All 73 KEV CVEs correctly identified as exploitation:active
  • Zero False Negatives - No missed KEV CVEs in dataset
  • 100% SSVC Coverage - All 295 CVEs have complete SSVC data

SSVC Score Calculation

In addition to priority tiers, we calculate a quantitative SSVC score (0-60) for fine-grained sorting within tiers: 

SSVC Score Formula:
  score = exploitation_weight + automatable_weight + impact_weight

Weights:
  Exploitation: active=30, poc=15, none=0
  Automatable: yes=15, no=0
  Technical Impact: total=15, partial=0

Score Ranges:
  60 points → A/Y/T (maximum risk, ACT tier)
  45 points → A/N/T or A/Y/P (ATTEND tier)
  30 points → A/N/P or P/Y/T (ATTEND tier)
  15 points → P/Y/P or P/N/T (TRACK tier)
  0 points  → N/N/P (minimum risk, TRACK tier)

Integration with Risk Scoring

SSVC complements (does not replace) our existing multi-factor risk scoring system. The final risk score (0-100) integrates:

  • SSVC Score (60% weight): Decision-focused prioritization from CISA framework
  • CVSS Score (25% weight): Technical severity and attack complexity
  • EPSS Score (15% weight): Exploitation probability prediction
Example: A vulnerability with CVSS 9.8, EPSS 85%, and SSVC A/Y/T (60) receives a risk score of: 
(9.8 × 2.5) + (85 × 0.15) + (60 × 0.6) = 24.5 + 12.75 + 36 = 73.25/100

5. Exploitation Status Detection

We detect exploitation status from multiple signals:

  • KNOWN_EXPLOITED: Confirmed exploitation (CISA KEV, exploit-db, Metasploit modules)
  • POC_AVAILABLE: Public proof-of-concept code exists (GitHub, security research)
  • EXPLOIT_LIKELY: EPSS ≥80% but no confirmed exploit yet
  • MONITORED: EPSS 60-79%, under surveillance

False Negative Analysis

Limitations: By focusing on EPSS ≥60%, we intentionally exclude:

  • Zero-day vulnerabilities: Newly disclosed CVEs without EPSS scores yet (typically added within 24-48 hours)
  • Targeted/APT exploits: Nation-state attacks with low EPSS due to limited telemetry visibility
  • Emerging threats: CVEs with rapidly increasing EPSS that haven't crossed 60% threshold yet
Mitigation: These edge cases are addressed through:
  • 4-hour update cycle (rapid inclusion of new high-EPSS CVEs)
  • CISA KEV monitoring (captures zero-days once confirmed exploited)
  • Manual review of trending CVEs in security community
